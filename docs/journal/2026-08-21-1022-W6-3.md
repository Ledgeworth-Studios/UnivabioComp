# W6-3 — The demo script, timed by counting rather than guessing

**Status:** DONE
**Files touched:** `docs/demo-script.md` (new), `README.md`, `BACKLOG.md`

## What I did

`docs/demo-script.md` is a shot-by-shot script for the submission video: seven
beats, what is on screen for each, the words to say over it, and how long each
one takes.

The arc is problem → the idea in one sentence → **the live run** → a real trap in
real data → the thing you walk away with → rigor → an honest close.

## Two things about it that are not decoration

### The timings are measured, not estimated

I wrote it, then counted the spoken words and divided by 150 a minute. My first
draft claimed three minutes and actually ran **3:41**. Beat 7 alone claimed
fifteen seconds and needed forty.

A script whose own timings are wrong is worse than no script, because you find out
on camera. So I trimmed about seventy words and then **changed the stated times to
match the measurement** rather than trimming until the numbers matched my
original guess. It now runs **3:13**, and says so, and says that dropping Beat 4
brings it to **2:52** if the limit turns out to be three minutes. Both figures are
computed, and the document contains the snippet that computes them, so anybody
rewriting a beat can re-measure in a second. I ran that snippet to check it works.

### It refuses to invent the video rules

The done-criteria I wrote for this task said to quote Devpost's rules on video
length. **I could not read them** — `univabio.devpost.com` and its `/rules` page
both return nothing to an automated fetch, presumably because they render client
side. Guessing at "three minutes" and stating it as a rule would have been worse
than useless.

So the first line of the document says the limit is **unconfirmed**, tells the
entrant to look it up, and gives a numbered cut order so the script can be
shortened without rewriting. I amended the backlog task to record the amendment
rather than quietly dropping the criterion.

## Why I did it this way

**Every figure spoken in the script names where it was verified.** The
ninety-one-site trial, the age flip, the health-centres study — each beat ends
with the journal entry that proved it. Registry data changes; the entrant can
re-check each claim before recording instead of taking a script's word for it.
There is also an instruction at the top to rehearse the whole thing on the day.

**There is a section called "things not to say."** Never *"you're eligible"*.
Do not describe the criteria judging as working — it is not built. Do not call
the interface accessible — contrast and keyboard paths were measured and fixed
but nothing has been tested with a screen reader. A demo video is where a
carefully hedged product gets oversold by an enthusiastic narrator, and the
places this one could go wrong are known and written down.

**The eval number gets a marked, optional insert rather than a blank.** The task
asked for "the eval number" and there isn't one — W4-2 needs the API key, and five
labels need a human review besides. A script with `__%` in the middle of it invites
somebody to fill it in with something plausible. Instead the main script works
without it, and there is a separate block, clearly labelled, showing exactly where
the sentence goes and what it should say **if** a real number exists — with a line
saying not to speak it until `just check` has produced one.

**Beats 2, 3, 5 and 7 are marked as uncuttable**: the idea, the proof, the
deliverable, and the honesty. Everything else has a cut number. If the entrant is
up against a limit at midnight, the decision is already made.
