## What I did

Under the results there is now a button — **"Take these to your appointment"** —
which switches the page to a clean summary built for paper. It prints the date,
what was searched with, the disclaimer, and then for each trial:

```
Study to Evaluate the Effectiveness and Safety of Ozanimod Compared to
Fingolimod in Children and Adolescents With Relapsing Remitting MS

NCT06408259 · RECRUITING · PHASE3 · Bristol-Myers Squibb

Nearest site: Local Institution - 0092, Portland, Oregon, United States
              — 0.6 miles (this study has 33 sites)

WHAT WOULD STOP YOU
This trial enrols ages 10 Years to 17 Years; you told us you are 41 years
old. (registry says: 10 Years to 17 Years)

Full record: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06408259
```

Four sections appear per trial when they have anything in them: *what would stop
you*, *not settled*, *ask the study team*, and any W3-4 caution about a study
that may not enrol individuals. A second button prints it or saves it as a PDF.

## Why I did it this way

**The scope of this page was a real decision, and W3-3's measurement forced it.**
The backlog called this a "printable page for the coordinator questions". But
W3-3 measured how many coordinator questions exist today: one, across 130 live
trials. The interesting unknowns are in the free-text criteria, and reading those
needs the judge, which is waiting on an API key. A page that printed only
questions would have come out blank almost every time — a feature that is
technically complete and practically useless.

So it prints what somebody actually needs at an appointment: which trials, where,
what would stop them, what nobody has settled, and what to ask. That is useful on
today's data. The "ask the study team" section is already in place, so when the
judge lands, W3-3b's output appears there without this page being touched.

**Two things are deliberately different from the on-screen card, and both are
because paper is not a browser.**

*Every URL is written out in full.* You cannot click a printout. A card that says
"Read this trial on ClinicalTrials.gov" as a link becomes a dead end on paper, so
the summary prints `https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06408259` as text a person
can type or read down a phone.

*Nothing is collapsed, so the criteria are left out entirely.* On screen the
eligibility criteria hide behind a disclosure triangle. Paper has no triangle —
they would all print, a dozen unread criteria per trial, burying the four lines
that are actually actionable. The registry address is printed instead. If a
person wants the full criteria they can get them from the source, which is also
rigor rule 5's whole argument.

**The printable view is a mode of the page, not a route.** Adding a router would
mean a new dependency, a new concept, and a new thing to explain to a judge, in
exchange for a URL nobody needs. A boolean and an early return do the same work
and are obvious on sight. It also guarantees what-you-see-is-what-prints, since
the same DOM is on screen and on the page.

**The date is passed in rather than read inside the component.** `new Date()`
inside `PrintableSummary` would make it a function that returns something
different every time it renders, which is exactly the sort of thing that is
impossible to test later. It is captured once, when the button is clicked.

**`@media print` hides the buttons rather than the component hiding them
itself.** The two controls carry a `no-print` class, and the print stylesheet
also drops backgrounds and page padding so a paper copy has no dead furniture and
does not waste ink. I verified this is genuinely in effect rather than merely
written — the rule is present in the browser's live stylesheet list with the
selectors it should have.
