# W5-2 — Layout down to 375px, and a word that was doing the wrong job

**Status:** DONE
**Files touched:** `web/src/profile.ts`, `web/src/ProfileChips.tsx`,
`web/src/profile.test.ts`, `web/src/index.css`, `BACKLOG.md`

## What I did

### The word first, because it matters more than the layout

With "Anywhere" selected, the *Within* chip read **"not said"**.

In this product "not said" is not a shrug. It means *we don't know, so this
becomes a question for the study team rather than a reason you were ruled out* —
and there is a sentence directly under the chips explaining exactly that. A search
with no location has no radius to state. Nobody failed to mention it. Using the
same phrase for "not applicable" quietly borrows the one idea the interface most
needs to keep sharp, in the one place it is being explained.

The chip now reads **"not needed"**. Each chip carries its own label for absence,
so the distinction is a property of the field rather than something the renderer
guesses, and there are tests on both cases.

### The layout

I measured before changing anything, which turned out to be worth doing — at
375px the page had **no horizontal overflow at all**, on the form, the results and
the printable summary. The pieces that could have broken it were already fine.
What was actually wrong was smaller:

- **Tap targets.** Several controls were 39–43px tall. Everything a finger has to
  hit is now at least 44px — the size a fingertip actually covers. The two
  exceptions are the chip's own value and its × , which sit inside a line of text
  where a 44px box would break the sentence around them; they get padding instead.
- **Long unbroken strings.** The printable summary prints full URLs, because paper
  cannot be clicked. `https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06655896` fits at 375px
  — and a longer identifier would not have. Registry facility names and identifiers
  now wrap rather than pushing a card off the screen.
- **Reading measure.** Explanatory text is capped at 62 characters a line instead
  of running the full width of a desktop window. Measured after: 625px on a 1280px
  viewport.
- **Below 480px**, cards and panels lose some padding, the two print buttons
  stack, and the heading comes down a size.

## Why I did it this way

**I measured overflow rather than looking for it.** The done-criteria say
`document.documentElement.scrollWidth <= window.innerWidth`, checked in the
running page, plus a sweep of every element for anything whose right edge is past
the viewport. A screenshot at 375px would have shown me a page that looked fine,
because a page with a 40px overflow *does* look fine until you scroll sideways.
Final numbers: scrollWidth 375 at 375, scrollWidth 1280 at 1280, zero overflowing
elements at either, zero controls under 44px.

**I did not redesign anything.** The measurement said the layout worked; the
faults were specific and small. Rewriting the visual design because the task is
called "typography and layout pass" would have thrown away work that a
measurement had just shown to be sound, and would have needed the whole of W5-1's
verification doing again.

## What the next run should know

1. **The next `READY` task in file order is W5-3, accessibility.** Some of its
   groundwork exists — form labels are tied to inputs, chip buttons carry
   `aria-label`s, the results region sets `aria-busy`, the error is `role="alert"`
   and the updating notice is `role="status"`. **None of it has been tested with
   an actual screen reader, and it must not be described as accessible until it
   has.** Colour contrast has not been checked at all; the muted grey
   (`--muted: #5a6b7d`) on the card background is the pairing most likely to fail,
   and it is used for every "registry says" line.
2. **The 44px rule has two deliberate exceptions**, both inside the chips, both
   commented at the CSS. If a future change makes chips their own row rather than
   inline text, those exceptions should go.
3. **20 frontend tests, 171 Python tests**, both gates green, verified at 375px
   and 1280px with overflow measured rather than eyeballed.
