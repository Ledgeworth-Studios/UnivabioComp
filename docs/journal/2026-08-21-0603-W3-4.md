## What I did

Here is a real registry entry:

> **Implementing Scalable, PAtient-centered, Team-based, Technology-enabled Care
> for Adults With Type 2 Diabetes (iPATH)** — recruiting, ages 18 and over, all
> sexes, healthy volunteers accepted.

Everything a search engine looks at says this is a diabetes trial for adults. It
is not. Read its eligibility criteria and the participants turn out to be
*federally qualified health centres*: "two largest clinic sites in multi-clinic
FQHCs", prioritising ones with poorer blood-sugar control, excluding ones with
fewer than 5,000 patients. A person with type 2 diabetes cannot enrol in it.

The card for that trial now opens with a caution:

> This study's eligibility criteria appear to describe clinics, practices or
> health systems rather than individual people, so it may not be one you can join
> as a patient. **We could be wrong** — the evidence is below, and the study team
> can tell you for certain.
>
> - The registry records this study's purpose as health services research.
>   *Record says: primary purpose: HEALTH_SERVICES_RESEARCH*
> - The eligibility criteria describe organisations, not individuals.
>   *Record says: Two largest clinic sites in multi-clinic FQHCs operating in or
>   near Massachusetts, Ohio, California, Puerto Rico*
> - The criteria describe the participant as having a patient population.
>   *Record says: FQHCs that have a patient population comprised of more than 80%
>   children*

On a live search of 40 recruiting type 2 diabetes trials, **exactly one** was
flagged, and it was that one.

## Why I did it this way

**The hazard here runs the opposite way from what you would guess, and the whole
design follows from that.** Flagging a study that does enrol people costs the
reader ten seconds of confusion. *Missing* one costs them nothing much either —
they read a trial they cannot join. But **wrongly telling somebody a study is not
for people, and hiding or burying it on that basis, costs them a trial they could
have joined and never heard about.** That is the same shape as a `NOT_MET` that
should have been `UNKNOWN`, the error `docs/PLAN.md` says to weight hardest. So:

**Two independent signals are required, never one.** Patient trials mention
buildings constantly — "currently hospitalised", "able to attend the study site",
"resident in a nursing home", "referred by a physician practice". Any of those
would trip a single-keyword rule, and all four are pinned as tests that must
*not* flag. Corroboration turns a coincidence into a pattern. Health services
research on its own is not enough either; plenty of it enrols patients, and there
is a test for that too.

**The word list is short because every entry had to survive one question:** could
this word appear in the criteria of a trial that enrols people? "Hospital" fails
it. "Site" fails it. What survives is wording that is hard to write about a
single human being — FQHC, health system, clinic site, participating practices —
plus a lovely tell: an individual person does not have a *patient population*.

**Flagged trials are labelled, and not hidden and not moved down the list.**
Written up as `docs/decisions/0003`. Ranking them down was tempting — it feels
tidier — and was rejected because it makes our mistakes quiet and the reader's
consequences loud. The evidence is printed on the card precisely so a reader who
thinks we are wrong can see why we thought it and overrule us.

**One structured field was added to the registry client:** `primary_purpose`,
read from `designModule.designInfo.primaryPurpose`. It was already being fetched
and simply not parsed. Preferring a structured field over text is the plan's
standing rule, and it is the strongest of the three signals.

**There is a test whose job is to become obsolete.**
`test_that_trial_looks_perfectly_ordinary_in_its_structured_fields` asserts that
`NCT06251323`'s age, sex and healthy-volunteer fields all look like a normal
adult trial. That test *is* the justification for this module existing. If the
registry ever adds a field stating the unit of enrolment, it starts failing, and
whoever sees it should delete this module and use the field instead.

## What the next run should know

1. **This module is a heuristic and says so out loud.** It is the least certain
   code in the project. If a judge asks "how do you know?", the honest answer is
   the one on the card: we don't, we have evidence, and we show it. Do not let it
   grow into something that hides trials without a new decision record.
2. **The next `READY` task in file order is W3-5**, the printable page for the
   coordinator questions. Note that W3-3's measurement matters for it: on today's
   data most trials have *no* open questions, because the interesting ones come
   from the criteria and the judge is blocked. A print page that is usually empty
   may be worth building anyway — the structure is the deliverable and W3-3b fills
   it — but decide that deliberately and write down which you chose.
3. **Adding a field to `Study` breaks every test that builds one by hand.** Three
   test modules construct `Study(...)` directly and all three needed the new
   field. That is a small tax and it is the right trade — the dataclass has no
   defaults, so a new field can never be silently forgotten — but expect it.
4. **159 tests, `just check` green, both CI jobs green**, verified in a browser on
   live data with no console errors.
