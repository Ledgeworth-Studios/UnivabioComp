## What I did

The profile could say four things about a person: their age, their sex, whether
they are a healthy volunteer, and the names of their conditions. Almost every
criterion that actually decides a trial needs more than that.

It now also holds **when they were diagnosed**, **what they are taking now**, and
**what they have taken before** — each as a chip you can fill in or clear, each
carried onto the printable sheet.

## How the three fields were chosen, and what was left out

The test each one had to pass: **would a person say this in a sentence about their
situation, unprompted?**

Not "would a trial want to know" — a trial would want to know fifty things. The
pipeline's first step is meant to be free text in, profile out, so a field nobody
says out loud is one that extraction can never fill. It would sit empty forever
and quietly make every criterion touching it `UNKNOWN`, which is exactly where we
already were.

*"I was diagnosed in 2019, I'm on ocrelizumab, I was on interferon before that"* is
how people describe a long-term condition. Those three pass.

**Rejected, and the reasoning matters more than the list:**

- **Lab values and disease scores** — EDSS, A1c. A trial cares enormously; a
  person does not volunteer them, and asking would turn this into a clinical
  intake form. Criteria needing them stay `UNKNOWN` and become questions for the
  study team, **which is the product working, not failing.**
- **Pregnancy, and anything a trial screens for that a stranger should not ask.**
- **A free-text "anything else" box.** Tempting, and it would collect real
  information — which nothing downstream could use without a model reading it. It
  would be a field with no consumer wearing the costume of a feature, and it would
  collect sensitive text this project has promised not to keep.

Written up as `docs/decisions/0008`, including the rule that the list is not meant
to grow: a fourth field has to pass the same test *and* find a consumer.

## The bug this exposed, which is the same bug in a new costume

The eval set reads its profiles from JSON. That reader named the four fields it
knew about, one at a time. Add three fields to the dataclass and **the reader
silently drops them** — the pair would be scored against a person missing the
exact detail it was written to test, and the eval would look perfectly healthy
while measuring the wrong thing.

This project keeps finding the same defect wearing different clothes: something
plausible that nothing actually reads. So the reader now has a named list of
profile fields, and there is a test asserting that list equals the dataclass's
fields — it fails the moment somebody adds a field and forgets. A misspelled field
name in a pair is now refused outright rather than ignored, because ignoring it
would test a person who is not the one described.

That guard is worth more than the three fields.

## Why I did it this way

**Every field has a consumer, checked.** The chips display and edit them, the
printable sheet carries them, and the eval set now uses them. There is a test that
walks every key of the profile and asserts it appears as a chip, so a field cannot
be added and left invisible.

**The eval set is measurably better for it.** It was 2 `MET`, 2 `NOT_MET`, 27
`UNKNOWN` — lopsided because the profile could not describe anybody. It is now
**35 pairs: 3, 3, 29**, and three of the new pairs turn on treatment history:

- Somebody who has had BRIUMVI, against *"Participants who have not received any
  BRIUMVI infusion prior to study start"* → `NOT_MET`, and no clinical judgement
  is involved: the drug is named in the criterion and named in the profile.
- Somebody on ocrelizumab against the same criterion → `MET`.
- Somebody on a disease-modifying therapy against *"No DMT started in the 4 weeks
  prior"* → still `UNKNOWN`, because a treatment list narrows it and does not
  settle it. **That pair is there on purpose**: a new field should not be assumed
  to answer everything it touches.

**The printable sheet gained a line** saying that anything not listed was not
stated and was treated as unknown rather than as grounds to rule anything out. A
coordinator will ask what the tool was told, and the answer should be on the sheet
rather than in a browser tab that was closed on the way to the appointment.

**A cleared treatment list is `[]`, not `[""]`.** Same rule as the null age,
restated because there are now more fields to get it wrong on, with tests.
